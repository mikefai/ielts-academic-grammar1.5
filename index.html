For Comma Splice Patterns: "You are connecting independent thoughts using only commas. Commas do not carry the structural weight to partition independent clauses. Use a semicolon, coordinate with a conjunction, or end the sentence with a period."
For Run-on Patterns: "You are fusing independent clauses together without transitional marking. This obscures logical progression. Try incorporating relative clauses or linking coordinate points with appropriate conjunctions."

Form-Meaning-Use Framework

Calibrating sentence boundaries for IELTS Band 7.0 Grammatical Range and Accuracy

Error Category The Grammatical Failure (Form) The Impact on Meaning How to Repair for Band 7.0 (Use)
Sentence Fragment A dependent clause (often starting with a subordinator like because, although, or since) stands alone without a main clause. The reader is left waiting for the result or main assertion, leaving the argument incomplete. Attach the fragment to an adjacent independent clause or supply the missing subject/verb.
Comma Splice Two independent clauses are separated only by a comma (often with conjunctive adverbs like however or therefore). Blurs the boundary between separate arguments, making thoughts bleed together. Use a semicolon, a coordinating conjunction (but, and, so), or split into two sentences.
Run-on Sentence Two independent clauses are fused together with zero punctuation or coordinating conjunctions. Forces the reader to parse the structural separation points manually, causing fatigue. Add standard boundary markers (punctuation) or construct a clear complex sentence structure.
Band 7.0 Standard A clean mix of simple, compound, and complex sentences with precise punctuation boundaries. The reader focuses entirely on the coherence of the academic argument. Vary structures consciously while maintaining absolute boundary control.

Band 4.0 → 5.0 Style (Over-coordination & Boundary Errors)

"Some people support nuclear energy, it is clean. And they think it reduces carbon emissions. But nuclear plants can have accidents, then dangerous radiation leaks out."

Why it fails:
  • Comma splice ("energy, it is")
  • Fragment starting with "And..."
  • Run-on sentence layout ("accidents, then dangerous...")

Band 7.0+ Style (Complex range & precise boundaries)

"Although nuclear energy is relatively clean, concerns persist because plants are vulnerable to accidents. If an accident occurs, dangerous radiation leaks into the local environment."

Key features:
  • Accurate complex clauses ("Although nuclear... clean, concerns...")
  • Subordinate conditions ("If an accident...")
  • Zero fragment or comma splice vulnerabilities.

Teacher Answer Key & Feedback Scripts

Note to Teachers:

This key contains standard rationales, correct answers, and suggested teacher feedback language to support adult candidates moving from Band 4.0 (partial control) to Band 7.0 (consistent control of complex and compound sentences).

Diagnostic & Notice Items

  • Item 1 (Diagnostic): Sentence Fragment.
    Rationale: 'Although' creates a dependent modifier clause; it stands alone here, which is a fragment. Connect it to a main clause.
  • Item 2 (Diagnostic): Comma Splice.
    Rationale: Fuses two independent clauses with only a comma. Add a coordinating conjunction or separate with a semicolon.
  • Item 3 (Diagnostic): Run-on Sentence.
    Rationale: Conjunctive adverbs like 'therefore' cannot connect clauses without a preceding semicolon or period.
  • Item 4 (Diagnostic): Sentence Fragment.
    Rationale: Lacks a main subject and verb structure, stranded in dependency.
  • Item 5 (Diagnostic): Correct structure.
    Rationale: A clean dependent clause introduced by 'While', followed by a separating comma and an independent clause.
  • Item 6 (Notice): Error at index 5 (comma splice on "rise,").
    Rationale: Comma splice immediately before the transition 'however'.
  • Item 7 (Notice): Error at index 0 ("Despite").
    Rationale: Prepositional phrase stranded as an independent clause.
  • Item 8 (Notice): Error at index 6 ("memory").
    Rationale: Run-on. Two independent thoughts fused with no boundary indicator.

Choice & Misconception Keys

  • Item 9 (Choose): B.
    Rationale: Uses 'Although' to form a complex sentence, avoiding a splice.
  • Item 10 (Choose): A.
    Rationale: Integrates the fragment with a comma and relative pronoun 'which'.
  • Item 11 (Choose): B.
    Rationale: Correct use of a semicolon before the conjunctive adverb 'therefore'.
  • Item 12 (Misconception): C.
    Rationale: Comma splices frequently occur when learners treat transition words as coordinators.
  • Item 13 (Misconception): B.
    Rationale: Subordinated structures are dependent regardless of length.
  • Item 14 (Misconception): B.
    Rationale: Joining independent clauses with coordinators requires a preceding comma in academic registers.

Repair, Produce & Guided Construction Models

  • Item 15 (Repair): "Tourism brings economic benefits; however, it can also damage local ecosystems." (Or using: ", but..." or "Although tourism...")
  • Item 16 (Repair): "Since organic farming avoids synthetic chemicals, it causes less harm to the environment."
  • Item 17 (Repair): "Robots are performing repetitive factory tasks; furthermore, they are highly efficient." (Or using: ", and they..." / "; they...")
  • Item 18 (Produce): "Although online education offers flexibility, it requires strong self-discipline."
  • Item 19 (Produce): "The research team faced budget cuts, but they completed the project on time."
  • Item 20 (Produce): "Governments should subsidize public transit, which would reduce urban traffic congestion."
  • Item 21 (Construction): "Although solar power is clean, it remains expensive to store."
  • Item 22 (Construction): "Public transport is subsidized; therefore, commuters continue to drive."
  • Item 23 (Construction): "The government introduced new scholarship programs, which helped low-income students attend university."
  • Item 24 (Apply): "...free because it benefits... budgets, so they cannot..." (or "; they cannot")
  • Item 25 (Apply): "...dwellers. They offer a place to relax, and people can..." (or "; people can")

Authentic Exit Tickets

  • Item 27: A (Starting sentences with coordinating conjunctions 'But' creates coordinates fragments in formal texts).
  • Item 28: B (Commas require coordinating conjunctions to join clauses; otherwise, they generate comma splices).
